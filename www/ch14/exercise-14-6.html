Threaded accessibility test

Press Ctrl+A to turn on accessibility. This page is intentionally long, so the screen reader talks for a while. While it is talking, scroll the page, hover the links below, and press Ctrl+S to move the accessibility focus. Everything should respond instantly, because speaking now happens on its own accessibility thread instead of the browser thread.

A browser does many jobs at once. It parses HTML, computes styles, lays out boxes, paints them, and reacts to every key press and mouse move. All of that work shares one browser thread. If any single job hogs that thread, the whole browser feels frozen.

Speech is a slow job. Reading a paragraph out loud takes many seconds, which is an eternity compared to a sixteen millisecond animation frame. Blocking the browser thread for seconds at a time makes scrolling stutter and clicks feel dead.

The fix is the same one used for networking and raster work: hand the slow job to a dedicated thread. The browser thread only enqueues the text to speak and moves on. The accessibility thread speaks one utterance at a time, in order, and waits for each to finish without holding anyone up.
